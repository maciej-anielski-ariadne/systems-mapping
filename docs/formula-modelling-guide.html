Two evidence questions, recorded separately

A link and a formula can refer to the same interaction while making different claims. The link status records evidence for the causal claim—whether the source really affects the target in the stated direction. The formula status records evidence for the mathematical form and its parameter values—whether this equation, shape and calibration reproduce the relationship usefully.

These statuses are informational only. They communicate the author’s evidence assessment; they do not enable, disable, weight or otherwise change a calculation. Every syntactically valid active rule runs regardless of its status.
StatusFor a linkFor a formula
UnspecifiedNo causal-evidence assessment has been recorded.No assessment of the mathematical form or parameters has been recorded.
HypothesisThe causal direction is proposed for exploration and still needs support.The equation or its parameters are a modelling hypothesis chosen for exploration.
SupportedRelevant evidence or domain reasoning supports the causal claim, without claiming full validation.Evidence or domain reasoning supports the mathematical form or parameter choices, without claiming calibration or validation.
CalibratedThe relationship’s magnitude has been fitted or tuned to observations; that fit alone does not establish causality.The mathematical form or parameter values have been fitted or tuned to observed data.
ValidatedThe causal claim has been assessed using the author’s stated validation design.The form and parameters have been assessed against separate data, cases or another stated validation check.
Important: an empirically Calibrated or Validated formula may coexist with a Hypothesis link. A model can predict an association well without demonstrating that changing the source causes the target to change. The app records the distinction; it does not judge the quality of the evidence or the validation design.

The CSV keeps the assessments separate. Formula evidence uses formula_evidence_status, formula_evidence_rationale, formula_evidence_source and formula_evidence_last_reviewed on a box. Causal evidence uses evidence_status, evidence_rationale, evidence_source and evidence_last_reviewed on a link. Use YYYY-MM-DD for review dates where possible; other imported text is preserved. Missing or unrecognised status values load as Unspecified.

The decision in one minute

  1. Is this a what-if input the user should move?Make the box Adjustable. Its scenario multiplier sets the box directly; it is not a causal equation.
  2. Is proportional sensitivity the right mathematical form?Use links and Strength. Strength says how many percent the target changes when the source changes by 1%; it may be hypothesised or empirically fitted, while the link’s causal status is recorded separately.
  3. Should proportional effects compound?Use Standard for independent effects; use Additive when their deviations should sum without compounding.
  4. Should the least-ready proportional prerequisite gate the target?Use Weakest link (combine=min).
  5. Does the interaction need an explicit shape in compatible absolute units?Use a formula for conversion, allocation, ratios, capacity, balances, thresholds, joint requirements or delayed feedback. The form may be an identity, a domain assumption or an empirically fitted relationship; record its evidence status honestly.
  6. Must every route respect a hard floor or ceiling?Use the box’s Lowest / Highest allowed values. Use clamp() when the bound belongs inside one formula.
Precedence: Adjustable scenario value → Formula → Combine / link strengths. A higher-precedence choice makes the lower one inactive.

When “just add a multiplier” is—and is not—appropriate

There are three different ideas that can sound like a multiplier. Keeping them separate prevents a plausible-looking but incorrect model.

Scenario input

Adjustable multiplier

150% means “set this external input to 1.5 times its starting value.” Use it to ask what-if questions about Analyst capacity, Port scanner capacity or another controllable input.

Appropriate when: the user is choosing a scenario level. It does not describe how one box causes another.
Causal sensitivity

Link Strength (elasticity)

A Strength of 0.35 means a 10% source increase produces roughly a 3.5% target increase around the baseline—not “multiply the target by 0.35.”

Appropriate when: the response is proportional around the baseline, whether hypothesised or fitted. It is poor for fixed capacities, shares, subtraction, thresholds or saturation.
Inside a formula

Fixed rate or conversion

questioning_operations * detection_rate is appropriate only if the rate has a defensible unit such as detections per operation.

Appropriate when: it is a measured, assumed or fitted conversion, share or unit factor. Store the rate as a named Constant, document its provenance, and record the formula’s evidence status.
Use a formula instead

Shape-changing relationship

A multiplier cannot express “whichever is smaller,” “part divided by whole,” “subtract removals,” “only above a threshold,” or “read the previous pass.”

Formula required when: the causal rule changes shape, units or timing rather than merely sensitivity.

Two shipped examples with different jobs

Analyst capacity is Adjustable. Its incoming arrows from Trafficking threat volume and Route volatility remain visible in Atlas, but Simulation pins Analyst capacity to its chosen scenario multiplier. Those incoming Strengths do not calculate it.

Trafficking threat volume actively uses Standard. The adaptive link from Illicit drug availability has Strength 0.12, so the current rule is:

80 × (illicit_drug_availability / 136)^0.12

This is sensible if the claim is a relative sensitivity around the baseline. It is not an accounting identity. If a sum, rate or other fitted absolute-value form better represents the available evidence, use that formula instead and keep the causal-evidence claim on the link separate.

When a one-word Combine rule is enough

Current option

Standard / multiplicative

baseline × ∏ (source / source_baseline)^strength

Use for independent relative effects that compound. Trafficking threat volume currently uses Standard for its adaptive input from Illicit drug availability. The present target has one active input, so no compounding occurs there.

Avoid when the intended or empirically fitted relationship is better represented as a count, flow, rate, share, threshold or physical constraint.

Current option

Additive

baseline × (1 + Σ strength × (source_ratio − 1))

Use when related percentage effects should contribute without compounding. Air-border seizure volume currently uses this rule for Air-border detections (0.35) plus four supporting inputs (0.16 each).

This does not add detections, referrals, operations and capacity indices. It adds their weighted deviations from baseline.

Possible redesign

Weakest link

baseline × min((source / source_baseline)^strength, …)

Operational skills training could be gated by Border officer headcount and Specialist training places if both are genuine prerequisites and the scarcest relative capacity should dominate.

The shipped target is currently Additive. Use Weakest link when “weakest prerequisite” is the intended causal story, and record whether that story is a Hypothesis, Supported or more strongly evidenced.

Formula patterns: identities, assumptions and fitted relationships

Every card below is a proposed modelling scenario, not current logic in the Border Force map. A scenario can be implemented as a clearly labelled Hypothesis, or its form and parameters can be empirically Calibrated or Validated. Implementation does not itself establish the causal claim shown by its links.

Conversion product

Operations into detections

air_passenger_activities_03
* questioning_detection_rate

Passenger questioning (operations/month) × detections per operation = Air-border detections (detections/month).

  • Add a named rate Constant with its source, assumption or calibration method.
  • Keep the incoming arrow from Passenger questioning.
  • Resolve existing canine, scanner and profile arrows: include them in the formula or remove them.
  • Record formula evidence separately from the causal status of the incoming link.
Joint requirement

Volume needs readiness and reliability

interventions
* officer_readiness_share
* intelligence_reliability_share

Use multiplication when every factor is required and shares lie between 0 and 1. A zero factor produces zero output.

  • Output has the same unit as interventions.
  • Readiness and reliability must be dimensionless shares.
  • Use bounds of 0–1 on those share boxes.
Ratio + clamp

Examination coverage

clamp(passengers_examined
/ total_air_passengers, 0, 1)

A part divided by the matching whole gives a share. The current map has Passengers examined but lacks total air passengers.

  • Add a denominator covering the same population and period.
  • Do not substitute International flight connectivity: it is a pressure index.
  • Decide how zero traffic should be interpreted; the engine fallback is 0.
Capacity constraint

Targets that can actually be boarded

min(credible_vessel_targets,
vessel_boarding_capacity)

The smaller same-unit quantity is the realised throughput. Link Strength alone cannot create a hard capacity ceiling.

  • Both inputs must use vessels/month.
  • The current map does not contain these two absolute quantities.
  • Do not directly compare capacity indices with operations/month.
Allocation

Split attempted importation by route

attempted_importation
* share_air

Use a share Constant to allocate one flow to a route. Parallel route shares should sum to 1 if they exhaust the total.

  • Add a same-period total flow box.
  • Add named route-share Constants.
  • Test that allocated routes sum back to the total.
Flow balance

Examined passengers without a detection

passengers_examined
- air_border_detections

Subtraction is valid only if detections are formally a subset of examined passengers over the same period.

  • Confirm the semantic subset relationship.
  • Harmonise units and reporting windows.
  • Use max(result, 0) only if a negative result is impossible by definition.
Threshold / floor

Work above routine capacity

max(container_scan_demand
- routine_scan_capacity, 0)

Use max() when nothing should happen below a threshold and only the excess matters.

  • Demand and capacity must share units.
  • Document a floor Constant as a policy value, domain assumption or fitted parameter—not a silent tuning patch.
  • Add a new “excess demand” box rather than changing the meaning of Container scanning.
Delayed feedback

Availability affects later price pressure

base_street_price
* (1 + price_sensitivity
* (delay(drug_market_outcomes_outcomes_01)
/ baseline_availability - 1))

This could express a lagged influence from Illicit drug availability to Street drug price.

  • Add baseline and sensitivity Constants.
  • Draw the missing incoming arrow from availability to price.
  • delay() means previous solver pass—not last month—and does not guarantee convergence.
Baseline invariant: evaluate the proposed formula using every box’s starting value. Its answer must equal the target’s starting value. If it does not, the model will visibly jump before the user changes a scenario.

Formula setup and review checklist

  • Why is an explicit mathematical form preferable here: identity, constraint, domain assumption, theory or empirical fit?
  • Are the formula status (form and parameters) and link status (causal claim) recorded separately?
  • Does the formula reproduce the target starting value at baseline?
  • Are addition, subtraction, comparison and division using compatible units?
  • Do numerator and denominator cover the same population and period?
  • Is every conversion rate or route share a named Constant with a description?
  • Does every referenced box have an incoming arrow to the formula target?
  • Are extra incoming arrows removed, or deliberately documented as descriptive?
  • Do shares that partition a total sum to 1?
  • Do bounds represent real constraints rather than arbitrary stabilisers?
  • Is delay() understood as solver state rather than calendar time?
  • Have low, baseline and high scenarios been checked for direction and scale?
  • Does the calculation breakdown in Simulation tell the intended causal story?

Limitations and owner decisions

The current index-based map does not yet contain empirical formulas. It supplies illustrative elasticities, indices and causal links, but no fitted formula forms or parameters. That is a description of this map, not a rule against empirical formulas: a future formula may be Calibrated or Validated from observations even while the corresponding causal link remains a Hypothesis.
  • The current map mixes pressure indices, capacity indices, operations/month, outputs/month and outcome indices. Link aggregation tolerates mixed source units because it works with ratios; direct formulas usually do not.
  • A formula target ignores incoming Strength values. Arrows still document its causal inputs, and every referenced box must still have an arrow.
  • Malformed or causally unlinked formulas are deactivated with load findings. An unknown identifier remains active but reads as 0; a referenced box without a starting value also reads as 0.
  • Division by zero produces 0 with a diagnostic trace. That is an engine safety fallback, not a domain assumption.
  • The effect label fixes direction: increases and enables are positive; decreases is negative. An explicit Strength supplies magnitude, and an opposite sign is normalised to the effect’s direction.
  • Sixty capability inputs are Adjustable, so the 120 links entering them affect map structure and Atlas exploration but not Simulation values while those boxes remain pinned.
  • delay() removes same-pass ordering ambiguity in a formula loop. It does not model real time and does not guarantee a stable loop.
  • Evidence statuses are author-supplied metadata. They do not change calculation order, formula precedence, link Strength or solver results.
